<h2>What happened</h2><p>The Pacific Maritime Association, the employers' group at the West Coast ports, and ILWU Local 26 <a href="https://mynewsla.com/business/2026/11/21/watchmen-reach-tentative-deal-averting-strike-at-la-long-beach-ports/?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=watchmen-s-tentative-deal-lifts-the-la-and-long-beach-strike-threat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">reached a tentative agreement</a> on 21 November 2026, according to Reuters as carried by MyNewsLA. It covers about 250 watchmen at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach. Their previous contract ran out at 12:01 a.m. that day, and talks had resumed on 20 November with a federal mediator. The reports do not give the hour the deal was reached.</p><p>The terms have not been released. The agreement needs a vote by Local 26 members, and the current contract is extended until that happens, <a href="https://www.freightwaves.com/news/tentative-union-deal-averts-strike-at-port-of-la-long-beach?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=watchmen-s-tentative-deal-lifts-the-la-and-long-beach-strike-threat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">FreightWaves reported</a>. No vote date has been given.</p><p>I wrote on 16 November that a deal or another extension would end the risk for now, or defer it. A deal is what came, but I did not forecast one, so I do not count this as a call I got right.</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>The risk was never the 250 watchmen on their own. They are about 2% of the ILWU workforce at the two ports, but they staff six container terminals, and the employers say those terminals handle about 85% of Long Beach cargo and 33% of Los Angeles cargo. The larger danger was that thousands of longshore workers, marine clerks and foremen, who were not part of the dispute, could have honoured a picket line, <a href="https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/tentative-deal-port-watchmen/3973800/?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=watchmen-s-tentative-deal-lifts-the-la-and-long-beach-strike-threat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">NBC Los Angeles reported</a>. That would have turned a small bargaining unit into a stoppage at the busiest container gateway in the US.</p><p>The tentative deal removes that for now. It does not remove it for good. Until members vote, the contract is only extended, and a rejected deal would put the dispute back where it was. I found nothing on how likely a rejection is, so I will not guess.</p><p>The cost of the settlement is also unknown. The employers' last reported offer before the deal was a one-time $3,200 bonus and a rise in average annual earnings for full-time watchmen from more than $170,000 to $198,000. It also kept employer-paid health care, worth more than $62,000 a year, and raised pensions to as much as $93,200 a year for the longest-serving workers. Reuters and NBC Los Angeles both say this was the last offer reported, not the confirmed deal. If the final terms differ, I cannot say how.</p><h2>Who it reaches</h2><p><b>Importers with cargo at Long Beach and Los Angeles terminals. Confidence: inferred.</b> A gate or picket stoppage would have kept containers waiting at terminals handling most of Long Beach's cargo. The tentative deal removes that for now, and the ratification vote is the remaining risk. I cannot see which importers use which terminals, so I name none. Sources: Reuters via MyNewsLA and FreightWaves, 21 November 2026.</p><p><b>Buyers and sellers of transpacific container space. Confidence: estimated.</b> Drewry named possible labour disruption at Los Angeles and Long Beach as a key risk in its 19 November assessment. It put Shanghai to Los Angeles at $7,026 per 40-foot container, down 5% and the lowest in 12 weeks, and expected rates to fall further the next week. That assessment came before the deal, so I cannot say how Drewry reads the risk now. My judgement is that one named upside risk to rates has eased, but it is a judgement from Drewry's wording, not something Drewry has said. Source: Drewry World Container Index, 19 November 2026.</p><p><b>Longshore workers, marine clerks and foremen at the two ports. Confidence: reported.</b> They were the group that could have stopped work behind a watchmen picket line, according to NBC Los Angeles and Reuters. Their own contract is separate and runs through July 2028, according to FreightWaves on 9 November. Source: NBC Los Angeles, 21 November 2026.</p><h2>What would change this view</h2><ol><li><p><b>The ratification vote.</b> A rejection would restore the strike risk. Watch statements from Local 26 and the employers' group, and Reuters. The date of the vote is the first thing to find out.</p></li><li><p><b>The published terms.</b> If the final agreement differs from the last reported offer, the cost and the likelihood of ratification both change. Watch for the employers' group or the union to release them.</p></li><li><p><b>Drewry's next assessment, due 26 November.</b> If Shanghai to Los Angeles keeps falling and the labour risk drops out of Drewry's list of key risks, that supports the view that the deal has eased pressure on rates. If rates rise instead, something other than labour is moving them.</p></li><li><p><b>November cargo counts and the next Global Port Tracker.</b> The National Retail Federation forecast 2.05 million twenty-foot container units for November at the ports it tracks.
