Photo: Don Ramey Logan / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.</p></span><h2>What happened</h2><p>On 10 November 2026 the Office of the US Trade Representative (USTR) <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/10/2026-22867/notice-of-modification-of-section-301-action-chinas-targeting-of-the-maritime-logistics-and-shipbuilding-sectors-for-dominance?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=us-keeps-port-fees-on-chinese-linked-ships-suspended-until-9-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">published a notice</a> in the Federal Register that further suspends its Section 301 action on China's maritime, logistics and shipbuilding sectors. The suspension runs from 9 November 2026 through 11:59 p.m. Eastern on 9 January 2027.</p><p>The action covers fees on certain maritime transport services, set out in Annexes I, II and III of the original April 2025 notice, and duties on ship-to-shore cranes and other cargo handling equipment from China, set out in Annex V.A of an October 2025 notice. The notice says that during the suspension "no party will accrue liability for or be required to pay" either.</p><p>This is a shorter extension than the last one. The previous suspension, announced after a US-China trade deal, ran for a year, from 10 November 2025 through 9 November 2026. USTR said it proposed the new two-month suspension on 23 October, received about 60 unique comments, and decided to continue discussions with China. It also said it will "consider whether it is appropriate to continue the suspension period or whether further action is appropriate" before the January deadline.</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>The fees are a cost on ship calls at US ports, so suspending them keeps a cost from reaching the shipping lines that would have owed it. Some commenters said a further suspension would avoid increased shipping costs and commercial disruption, according to the notice. That is the reason to watch the date: when a fee returns, the usual route is through the price of moving cargo, either as a carrier surcharge or as ships being moved onto other services (inferred).</p><img alt="Aerial view of the Long Beach waterfront, marina and port cranes" src="https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/96db15cd-7e17-4c42-983e-aff8053467ca/image.jpg?t=1794971061" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><span><p>Long Beach waterfront and port cranes, 2020. Photo: Don Ramey Logan / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.</p></span><p>The change from one year to two months matters as much as the extension itself. A shipper planning a contract or a sailing programme can no longer assume the fees stay off for a year. The window now closes on 9 January 2027, the same day the 178 Section 301 tariff exclusions on Chinese goods <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/10/2026-22925/notice-of-product-exclusion-extensions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=us-keeps-port-fees-on-chinese-linked-ships-suspended-until-9-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">were extended to</a>, so two separate US-China decisions now fall due together (reported).</p><p>My reading is that USTR is holding the fees in reserve while talks continue, and renewing in short steps so it can reverse (estimated). The notice does not say this. It says the suspension allows further discussion with China, and it leaves further action open.</p><h2>Who it reaches</h2><p><b>Operators of vessels covered by Annexes I to III (reported).</b> The notice says no party accrues liability for the maritime fees during the suspension. I have not read the annexes' vessel definitions, so I cannot say which lines or ships are covered, and I am not naming any. Source: USTR notice, 10 November 2026.</p><p><b>Importers and exporters that buy ocean freight (inferred).</b> Commenters told USTR that continued suspension would avoid higher shipping costs. If the fees return in January, the cost would reach cargo owners through freight rates or surcharges. How much, and when, depends on the fee amounts and on carriers' choices, neither of which I can estimate from the notice. Source: USTR notice, 10 November 2026.</p><p><b>Port and terminal operators buying cranes and cargo handling equipment (inferred).</b> The Annex V.A duties on ship-to-shore cranes and other equipment from China are suspended too. The notice says comments on those duties generally supported ending the suspension and treating tariffs separately from the service fees. A separate decision on the duties is possible. Source: USTR notice, 10 November 2026.</p><h2>What would change this view</h2><ol><li><p><b>A further notice before 10 January 2027.</b> USTR says it will review before the deadline. A notice that lifts the suspension, extends it again or ends the fees permanently would settle the question. Watch the <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=us-keeps-port-fees-on-chinese-linked-ships-suspended-until-9-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Federal Register</a> and the <a href="https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=us-keeps-port-fees-on-chinese-linked-ships-suspended-until-9-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">USTR press office</a>.</p></li><li><p><b>Progress in US-China talks.</b> The suspension is tied to discussions with China. The notice says nothing about the terms, so announcements about the talks would be the signal.</p></li><li><p><b>A split between fees and crane duties.</b> Commenters asked for the crane duties to be handled separately. A notice that treats them differently from the service fees would show the two are being decoupled.</p></li><li><p><b>How lines price January sailings.</b> If carriers add fee-related surcharges to bookings for dates after 9 January, they expect the fees to return.
