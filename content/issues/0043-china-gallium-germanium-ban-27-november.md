<p><b>Update, 24 November 2026: </b>China has extended the suspension to 27 January 2027, according to reports by <a href="https://en.sedaily.com/world/2026/11/24/china-extends-suspension-of-gallium-export-curbs-ahead-of?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Seoul Economic Daily</a> and <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/beijing/china-extends-suspension-of-select-export-restrictions-on-us-by-two-months?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Tribune</a>. The headline above reflects the position when this was published. See <a href="https://alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com/p/china-extends-gallium-germanium-pause-january-2027?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">the follow-up</a> for what the extension changes.</p><h2>What happened</h2><p>China's pause on its ban on exports of gallium, germanium and antimony to the United States is due to end on 27 November 2026. The ban dates from 3 December 2024, when Beijing <a href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/china-bans-exports-to-u-s-of-key-high-tech-materials-in-response-to-chip-sanctions?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">answered new US chip controls</a> by barring these materials, and superhard materials, from going to the US. On 9 November 2025 it <a href="https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/china-halts-ban-on-gallium-germanium-antimony-exports-to-us-but-controls-remain-2025-11-10?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">suspended the ban for a year</a>.</p><p>The pause was narrow. It <a href="https://www.procyonmetals.com/en/blog/mofcoms-27-november-2026-gallium-germanium-and-antimony-deadline-a-us-specific-pause-not-deregulation?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">suspended only the ban's US-specific clause</a>. Export licences, which China has required for gallium and germanium since August 2023, stayed. So did the bar on supplying US military users.</p><p>China's 9 November 2026 notice <a href="https://www.skillings.net/china-extends-october-export-control-pause/?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">extended six other measures to 10 January 2027</a> but not this one. Talks between Trump and Xi in Shenzhen on 19 November <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/stalemates-over-semiconductors-and-rare-earth-minerals-appear-little-changed-as-trump-heads-home-from-china-121555352.html?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ended with little change on export controls</a>, Yahoo Finance reported. As of 23 November I have found no report of an extension. A lapse needs no announcement, so silence is not a signal either way (inferred).</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>Gallium is the clearer exposure. The US Geological Survey (USGS) says the US has recovered no primary gallium since 1987 and imports all of its supply. China produced 99% of the world's primary low-purity gallium. The USGS also lists where US gallium metal came from in 2021 to 2024: Canada 28%, Japan 22%, China 18%, Germany 16%. China's direct share is under a fifth, but the other suppliers largely sit downstream of Chinese primary output, so I expect a Chinese lapse to reach them as well (inferred; the USGS does not say where those suppliers get their feed).</p><p>Germanium is more direct. The USGS puts US net import reliance above 50% and says China supplied 41% of the germanium metal imported in 2021 to 2024, and 23% of all germanium imports once dioxide is counted. It estimates US germanium metal imports fell 67% in 2025, to 7,000 kg, and attributes that to China's ban. The average price of germanium metal it reports rose from $1,991 a kilogram in 2024 to $4,100 in 2025. The USGS does not tie the price rise to the ban, so I won't either.</p><p>The data also shows what we cannot yet read. US gallium metal imports more than doubled in 2025, to an estimated 25,000 kg, while consumer stocks at year end stayed near 3,400 kg. The USGS does not say why. I cannot tell whether buyers stocked up during the pause.</p><p>My reading is that China left this one out of the 9 November extension on purpose, as a lever it can pull at no cost (estimated). The notice does not say so. Beijing has also <a href="https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/stake-trump-xi-shenzhen-trade-111443590.html?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-s-us-ban-on-gallium-and-germanium-is-due-back-on-27-november" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">threatened countermeasures</a> over a new US chip rule issued on 17 November, and letting the pause lapse would be an easy reply.</p><h2>Who it reaches</h2><p><b>US makers of gallium arsenide and gallium nitride chips and optoelectronics (inferred).</b> The USGS says integrated circuits took 73% of US gallium consumption and optoelectronic devices 26%. A lapse would return Chinese gallium to the licence-and-ban regime that applied in 2025. I have no source tying a named company to the lapse, so I am not naming any.</p><p><b>Fibre-optic and infrared-optics makers (inferred).</b> The USGS lists fibre optics as the largest US use of germanium, then infrared optics, then semiconductors and solar cells. If Chinese metal is blocked again, buyers would depend more on Belgium, Germany and Canada, which the USGS lists as other import sources.</p><p><b>US aerospace and defence suppliers (reported, for the military-user bar).</b> The ban on supplying US military users stayed in place during the pause, according to the Reuters report of the suspension. I have found no company-level reporting, so I am naming none.</p><h2>What would change this view</h2><ol><li><p><b>A Ministry of Commerce notice before 27 November.</b> An extension, or confirmation that the ban resumes, would settle the date. Without one, the default is a lapse.</p></li><li><p><b>Licence approvals.</b> Gallium and germanium have needed Chinese licences since August 2023. Reports of long waits or refusals would show the pause was worth less than it looked, even before it ends.</p></li><li><p><b>Prices outside China.</b> European assessments of gallium and germanium from Argus or Fastmarkets would show whether buyers expect a lapse. I have no November 2026 price, so I am not putting one on the page.</p></li><li><p><b>The 10 January 2027 deadlines.</b> China's other measures run to 10 January, and the US port-fee suspension runs to 9 January. If the gallium ban returns first, it would be the one step in this truce not carried forward.</p></li></ol><h2>Sources</h2><ol><li><p>Gallium.
