<h2>What happened</h2>
<p>US employers added 29,000 jobs in September 2026, the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) said in its <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Employment Situation report</a> on 2 October. The report revised July to a loss of 10,000 jobs, from a gain of 21,000 in the previous estimate, and August to a gain of 133,000, from 162,000. Over the three months to September, payrolls rose by an average of 51,000 a month. The unemployment rate was 4.2%, within the range of 4.1% to 4.3% it has held since March, and the labour force participation rate was 61.8%, little changed since January.</p>

<p>Inflation is running above pay growth. The consumer price index rose 3.4% in the 12 months to August, the BLS said in its <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_09112026.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">August CPI report</a>, with energy prices up 16.3%, gasoline up 27.4% and airline fares up 23.4%. Excluding food and energy, prices rose 2.4%, slightly less than the 2.5% of July. Average hourly earnings rose 3.1% in the year to August and 3.0% in the year to September. The <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">September CPI report</a>, published on 14 October, showed prices 3.7% higher than a year earlier, with energy up 20.4%, gasoline up 34.2% and airline fares up 22.4%; excluding food and energy, prices rose 2.6%.</p>
<p>In its <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/realer_09112026.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">real earnings release</a> for August, the BLS said real average hourly earnings, which adjust pay for CPI inflation, were 0.3% lower than in August 2025. As first published, real pay was 1.4% higher than a year earlier in February, the month the US and Israeli strikes on Iran began (on 28 February), and 0.3% higher in March. It was below its year-earlier level in April (down 0.3%), May (down 0.7%), July (down 0.2%) and August (down 0.3%), and 0.1% above it in June. In September it was 0.7% lower than a year earlier, according to the <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/realer_10142026.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">real earnings release</a> of 14 October.</p>

<h2>Why it matters</h2>
<p>The pattern fits an energy shock rather than an overheating economy (inferred). Prices tied to fuel have risen sharply, while core inflation, which excludes food and energy, eased to 2.4% in August from 2.5% in July before rising to 2.6% in September. Higher fuel bills act like a tax on households, leaving less to spend on other goods (inferred). If the squeeze continues, it would reach imported consumer goods, and through them the container lines and ports that serve US importers (inferred).</p>
<p>It also bears on the Federal Reserve's position. The Fed raised rates on 16 September, and Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh said its aim was to stop changes in relative prices, such as oil, broadening into second- and third-order effects in the economy, according to the <a href="https://www.federalreserve.gov/mediacenter/files/FOMCpresconf20260916.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">press conference transcript</a>. Annual wage growth has slowed rather than accelerated, from 3.7% in the January report to 3.0% in September, as first published. The wage data show no sign yet of the energy shock feeding back into pay (inferred).</p>
<p>The revisions matter too. The 2 October report cut its previous estimates for July and August by a combined 60,000 jobs. Against the first estimates, job gains for June to August are now 42,000 lower: 154,000 in total, against 196,000 first reported.</p>
<h2>Who it reaches</h2>
<p><em>Transport and warehousing workers (sector).</em> Employment in transportation and warehousing was little changed in September, with a gain of about 7,600 jobs. Confidence: reported. Source: BLS, 2 October 2026.</p>
<p><em>Manufacturing (sector).</em> Factory employment was little changed in September (+9,000) but is up 72,000 since a low in December 2025, the BLS said, with plastics and rubber products and machinery each adding 5,000 jobs in September. Confidence: reported. Source: BLS, 2 October 2026.</p>
<p><em>Airlines and travellers (sector).</em> Airline fares in the CPI rose 23.4% in the year to August and 22.4% in the year to September. Confidence: reported for fares, inferred for the link to fuel costs. Source: BLS, 11 September and 14 October 2026.</p>
<h2>What would change this view</h2>
<ul>
<li><em>Core inflation.</em> A rise above 2.4% would suggest energy costs are spreading into other prices. The September reading, published on 14 October, was 2.6%.</li>
<li><em>The October jobs report, due on 6 November.</em> Another month of near-zero payroll growth, or an unemployment rate above 4.3%, would point to a weakening labour market.</li>
<li><em>Revisions.</em> The 2 October report lowered July and August by a combined 60,000 jobs. Further downward revisions would weaken the picture of the summer.</li>
</ul>
