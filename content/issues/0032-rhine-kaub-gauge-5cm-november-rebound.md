Kaub is the reference gauge near Koblenz that decides how heavily barges can load on the Middle Rhine. The reading stayed at or below 10 cm for about 15 hours, from 17:00 on 7 November to 07:45 on 8 November (Central European Time), and sat at 5 cm from 23:45 to 03:00.</p><img alt="The Kaub gauge tower beside the Rhine, its display reading 053, with a hillside castle behind" src="https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/b6658d1e-c9a5-4fdc-b83c-913e250b449b/rhine-2-kaub-gauge-tower-2018.jpg?t=1794533571" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><span><p>The Kaub gauge tower reading 53 cm on 13 October 2018, during that year's low water. File photo, 2018. Photo: Marion Halft / <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kaub_Pegel_Niedrigwasser_2018.jpg?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wikimedia Commons</a>, <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CC BY-SA 4.0</a>.</p></span><p>It has recovered since. The gauge read 25 cm at midnight on 12 November, 39 cm at noon that day, and 60 cm at 02:15 on 13 November.</p><p>Both numbers need context. The authority's reference values for 2010 to 2020 put the lowest daily level at 25 cm, the mean low-water level at 65 cm and the mean daily level at 208 cm. The previous recorded low before this summer was 25 cm, in 2018, <a href="https://www.bairdmaritime.com/shipping/inland-shipping/rhine-river-levels-continue-to-drop-hitting-new-all-time-low?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Reuters reported on 6 August</a>. <a href="https://mykn.kuehne-nagel.com/news/article/rhine-water-levels-recover-after-weekend-rain?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Kuehne+Nagel, citing the same authority</a>, put the August low at roughly 5 to 6 cm. So November matched that low, three months later.</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>How much a barge can carry depends on how deep the channel is. Kuehne+Nagel says vessels generally need a reading of about 150 cm at Kaub to sail fully loaded. At 60 cm, loads are still a fraction of that. In August, when the gauge was at 19 cm, HGK Shipping told Reuters its vessels could carry only about 20% of the tonnage that would normally be possible. When the gauge had recovered to about 75 cm on 25 August, Kuehne+Nagel reported loads of roughly 700 to 800 tonnes.</p><img alt="Panorama of Kaub with Pfalzgrafenstein castle on its island and exposed gravel banks beside it at low water" src="https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/eb7d33c1-183f-473c-b3e6-5b10e05e7d6d/rhine-3-kaub-pfalzgrafenstein-aug2026.jpg?t=1794533584" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><span><p>Kaub and Pfalzgrafenstein castle with gravel banks exposed during the August 2026 low water. File photo, 8 August 2026; a panorama of five shots. Photo: Rolf Kranz / <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Niedrigwasser_Mittelrhein_2026_-_Kaub_und_Burg_Pfalzgrafenstein.jpg?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wikimedia Commons</a>, <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CC BY-SA 4.0</a>.</p></span><p>Smaller loads mean the same cargo needs more boats. Reuters reported in August that loads were being spread across several part-loaded vessels, which adds cost, and commodity traders told it that tanker-barge freight from Rotterdam to Karlsruhe had risen to about €160 to €165 a tonne from €45 at the end of June. I have no November rate to compare, so I am not putting a current price on this.</p><p>The pattern is the point. The river has now fallen to about the same low twice in three months, with a partial recovery in between. A reading of 60 cm is well under 150 cm and under the 65 cm mean low-water level. I think a river that low is one dry spell away from the same problem (estimated). That is my reading, not a forecast from the authority.</p><h2>Who it reaches</h2><p><b>Barge operators and the shippers who use them for fuels, chemicals and bulk commodities (reported).</b> Reuters describes the Rhine as an important route for grain, minerals, ores, coal and oil products, and says operators add shallow-water surcharges when ships sail part-loaded, which raises costs for cargo owners. Source: <a href="https://www.bairdmaritime.com/shipping/inland-shipping/rhine-river-levels-continue-to-drop-hitting-new-all-time-low?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Reuters via Baird Maritime</a>, 6 August 2026.</p><p><b>Steelmakers that depend on barge deliveries of raw materials (reported, July).</b> Thyssenkrupp Steel told Reuters in July that low water had slightly reduced blast furnace production at Duisburg and that its own barge operations were suspended while it chartered shallower-draught vessels. I have no November statement from the company, so this is the August-era evidence, not a current claim. Source: <a href="https://www.bairdmaritime.com/shipping/inland-shipping/rhine-shipping-disruptions-threaten-fragile-german-economic-recovery?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Reuters via Baird Maritime</a>, 14 July 2026.</p><p><b>Industrial buyers who shift cargo to rail and road (reported, August).</b> Kuehne+Nagel said some freight, particularly bulk commodities, chemicals and fuels, had moved to rail and road, where capacity was also under pressure. That shift costs more and strains other routes. Source: <a href="https://mykn.kuehne-nagel.com/news/article/rhine-water-levels-recover-after-weekend-rain?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=rhine-at-kaub-fell-to-5-cm-again-then-rose-to-60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Kuehne+Nagel</a>, 25 August 2026.</p><p>I am not naming other companies. I have no source tying a named firm to this month's low.</p><h2>What would change this view</h2><ol><li><p><b>The gauge itself.</b> The authority publishes Kaub readings every 15 minutes. A sustained climb toward 150 cm would mean full loads are possible again. A fall back below 25 cm would repeat the August problem.</p></li><li><p><b>Rainfall in the Rhine catchment.</b> The level depends on rain upstream. The German Federal Institute of Hydrology (BfG) publishes water-level forecasts, which would show whether this recovery lasts.</p></li><li><p><b>Operators' shallow-water surcharges.</b> If barge operators keep or raise their low-water surcharges, that shows cargo owners are still paying for part-loaded boats.</p></li><li><p><b>Rail and road capacity.</b> Kuehne+Nagel noted capacity on those routes was already tight.
