<img alt="Novus Data" src="https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/e09786cc-4922-4d54-aa62-d3e47407148f/novus-data-logo-horizontal-600w-transparent.png?t=1794437685" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><h2>What happened</h2><p>On 24 November 2026, three days before the date, China extended its pause on the ban on exports of gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the United States. Two outlets report it. <a href="https://en.sedaily.com/world/2026/11/24/china-extends-suspension-of-gallium-export-curbs-ahead-of?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-extends-its-us-gallium-and-germanium-pause-to-27-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Seoul Economic Daily</a> says the Ministry of Commerce suspended the clause under which dual-use exports of these materials to the US would "in principle not be approved", from 28 November to 27 January 2027. The <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/beijing/china-extends-suspension-of-select-export-restrictions-on-us-by-two-months?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-extends-its-us-gallium-and-germanium-pause-to-27-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Tribune, relaying ANI and the South China Morning Post</a>, gives the same new end date, up from 27 November. The ministry's own notice, <a href="https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/blgg/gg/2026/art/2026/art_fc4036b0e6b046e0a84c5579ce09e5b0.html?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-extends-its-us-gallium-and-germanium-pause-to-27-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Announcement No. 59 of 2026</a>, confirms the date and the suspension of the clause. It does not mention licences or the military bar, so those two points rest on the press reports.</p><p>Seoul Economic Daily also says the bar on dual-use exports to the US military, or for military purposes, remains in place. The Tribune does not address it. China first imposed the ban on 3 December 2024, and on 9 November 2025 it <a href="https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/china-halts-ban-on-gallium-germanium-antimony-exports-to-us-but-controls-remain-2025-11-10?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-extends-its-us-gallium-and-germanium-pause-to-27-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">suspended it for a year</a>.</p><p>Both outlets link the move to Xi Jinping's planned US visit. Seoul Economic Daily adds that the two leaders are due to meet again at the G20 summit in Miami on 14 and 15 December.</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>On Monday I wrote that without a notice before 27 November the default was a lapse, and that China had probably left this ban out of its 9 November extension on purpose, as a lever (estimated). The extension came instead. I do not know why this ban was handled separately from the six measures extended on 9 November. The timing, ahead of a leaders' meeting, fits a gesture before talks, but that is my reading and neither report says it (estimated).</p><p>The extension is narrow in the same way the original pause was. It suspends the US-specific clause. It does not touch the export licences China has required for gallium and germanium since August 2023, and it does not lift the military-user bar. Gallium and germanium therefore still reach US buyers only if Chinese licences are approved. The <a href="https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-germanium.pdf?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-extends-its-us-gallium-and-germanium-pause-to-27-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">US Geological Survey</a> estimates that US germanium metal imports fell 67% in 2025, to 7,000 kg, and attributes that to China's ban. The ban was only suspended in November 2025, so that fall shows what the ban did and says nothing yet about what the pause allows.</p><p>The new date also changes the calendar. China's six other measures run to 10 January 2027, and <a href="https://alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com/p/us-port-fees-chinese-ships-suspended-january-2027?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-extends-its-us-gallium-and-germanium-pause-to-27-january" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">the US suspension of port fees on Chinese-linked ships</a> runs to 9 January. The gallium deadline now falls after both, so the truce has a cluster of dates in January and this one is the last.</p><h2>Who it reaches</h2><p><b>US makers of gallium arsenide and gallium nitride chips and optoelectronics (inferred).</b> The USGS says integrated circuits took 73% of US gallium consumption and optoelectronic devices 26%. The extension keeps the no-approval clause suspended for two more months, which removes the risk of an automatic ban on 27 November. I have no source tying a named company to this change, so I name none.</p><p><b>Fibre-optic and infrared-optics makers (inferred).</b> The USGS lists fibre optics as the largest US use of germanium, then infrared optics, then semiconductors and solar cells. The licence requirement still applies, so supply stays dependent on approvals.</p><p><b>US aerospace and defence suppliers (reported, for the military-user bar).</b> According to Seoul Economic Daily, the bar on dual-use exports to the US military remains. The extension gives these buyers nothing. I have found no company-level reporting, so I name none.</p><h2>What would change this view</h2><ol><li><p><b>The Ministry of Commerce notice.</b> Its wording should confirm the end date, and show whether the licence rule and the military bar are untouched. If it differs from the two reports, this article is wrong and I will say so.</p></li><li><p><b>Licence approvals.</b> Reports of long waits or refusals would show the pause is worth less than it looks.</p></li><li><p><b>Xi's US visit and the 14 and 15 December G20 meeting.</b> A breakdown in talks would raise the chance that the 27 January date is not renewed.</p></li><li><p><b>The January dates.</b> If China's 10 January measures or the US port-fee suspension move, this one is likely to move with them.</p></li></ol><h2>Sources</h2><ol><li><p>China extends suspension of gallium export curbs ahead of Xi's US trip.
