<h2>What happened</h2><p>On 20 August 2026 the Panama Canal Authority cut daily booking slots, citing rainfall across the canal watershed that was 34% below the historical average from May to August, with inflows 44% below. From booking dates starting 4 September it set the Neopanamax locks (the larger locks) to nine slots a day and the Panamax locks (the older, narrower locks) to 25. It also said a potentially severe 2026-2027 El Niño raised concern about water for the dry season, which runs from January to April 2027.</p><p>On 28 September it eased the limits. It added a tenth Neopanamax slot from booking dates starting 15 October, bringing total capacity to 33 transit slots a day, and set the maximum Neopanamax draft at 14.94 metres (49.0 feet), citing improving water conditions and the projected level of Gatun Lake.</p><p>On 21 October it reinstated last-minute reservations at the Panamax locks, citing close-to-average recent rainfall. The same advisory said the water deficit persists, that the booking system remains the only way to guarantee a transit date, and that vessels without a reservation "may experience extremely extensive delays."</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>The canal limits traffic in two ways: the number of ships it lets through each day, and how deep they can sit in the water. More slots means more transits. A greater allowed draft means the largest ships can load more cargo before they cross. The October changes add some of both, which is why I read them as an easing and not as a return to normal (inferred).</p><p>The limit that still binds is planning. Because a booking is the only guarantee, a shipper that cannot get a slot has to accept a wait of uncertain length or reroute. That cost lands on the services that depend on the canal for a fixed schedule, such as container lines running Asia to the US East Coast, more than on ships that can choose another route (inferred).</p><p>The second thing to watch is timing. The authority linked its August cuts to forecasts for the 2027 dry season, and its October advisory says the deficit persists. If the wet season ends with the lake lower than the authority wants, I would expect it to tighten again before or during January to April (estimated). Nothing in the advisories says this will happen. It is my reading of how the authority has acted so far.</p><h2>Who it reaches</h2><p><b>Container shipping (inferred).</b> Advisory A-36-2026 guarantees full-container vessels at least 42 Neopanamax slots a week, and the deeper draft lets the largest ships load closer to capacity. That reduces the pressure on lines using the canal this autumn. Source: Advisory A-36-2026, Panama Canal Authority, 28 September 2026.</p><p><b>Panamax bulk and tanker operators (inferred).</b> The October increase applies to the Neopanamax locks only. Total capacity of 33 slots, with 10 at the Neopanamax locks, leaves 23 at the Panamax locks, two fewer than the 25 set in August. On 21 October the authority reinstated last-minute reservations at the Panamax locks, offered when a slot becomes available and at the authority's discretion. Sources: A-36-2026 and A-39-2026.</p><p><b>Vessels without a booking (reported).</b> The authority itself says they may face "extremely extensive delays" while the water deficit persists. Source: A-39-2026, 21 October 2026.</p><p>I am not naming individual carriers or companies. The advisories describe vessel categories, not operators, and I have no source tying a named company to these limits.</p><h2>What would change this view</h2><ol><li><p><b>A new advisory.</b> The authority publishes advisories to shipping at <a href="https://pancanal.com?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=panama-canal-eases-limits-but-warns-of-a-2027-dry-season-risk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">pancanal.com</a>. A cut to slots or draft would show the easing is reversing, and further added slots would strengthen it.</p></li><li><p><b>The size of the water deficit.</b> Advisory A-29-2026 gave rainfall at 34% below average and inflows 44% below for May to August. A later advisory citing figures closer to average would weaken the dry-season concern.</p></li><li><p><b>The El Niño outlook.</b> The authority tied its concern to the forecast severity of the 2026-2027 El Niño. A weaker forecast would weigh against my estimate of renewed limits.</p></li><li><p><b>Waiting times for vessels without a booking.</b> If unbooked ships are in practice waiting no longer than before, the authority's warning is more cautious than conditions. Reports from shipping agents would show this.</p></li></ol><h2>Sources</h2><ol><li><p>Advisory to Shipping No. A-29-2026: Additional Measures to Address Reduced Precipitation in the Canal Watershed.
