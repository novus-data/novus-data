<h2>What happened</h2>
<p>The Drewry World Container Index (WCI), a weekly measure of spot rates on eight East-West routes, fell 2% to $4,351 per 40ft container on 8 October 2026, according to Drewry's assessment as <a href="https://www.thedcn.com.au/news/world-container-index-8-october-2026" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">reported by DCN</a>. On 9 October 2025 the same index was $1,651, after 17 straight weekly declines and at its lowest since January 2024, according to <a href="https://www.mtsinsights.com/summaries/5268/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">MTS Insights</a>. Since early August 2026 it has stayed between $4,297, on 6 August, and $4,526, on 20 August, according to MTS Insights summaries of <a href="https://www.mtsinsights.com/summaries/10326/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">6 August</a> and <a href="https://www.mtsinsights.com/summaries/10554/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">20 August</a>.</p>
<p>The average hides a wide spread. On 8 October a 40ft container from Shanghai to New York cost $10,220 on the spot market, and Shanghai to Los Angeles $7,624. Shanghai to Rotterdam was $3,337 and Shanghai to Genoa $3,696. A week earlier, Drewry said Asia-Europe rates had fallen for 12 consecutive weeks, and that "increasing vessel transits through the Suez Canal are adding effective capacity to the trade", according to <a href="https://www.thedcn.com.au/news/world-container-index-1-october-2026" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">DCN</a>.</p>

<p>Carriers' own figures moved far less. Hapag-Lloyd reported an average freight rate of $1,475 per twenty-foot equivalent unit (TEU) in the second quarter of 2026, up from $1,354 a year earlier, in its <a href="https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/content/dam/website/downloads/ir/HLAG_Investor_Report_H1_2026.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">half-year report</a> of 13 August 2026. Its first-half average was $1,406, almost unchanged from $1,411. A.P. Moller-Maersk <a href="https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2026/08/13/maersk-delivers-strong-q2-and-raises-full-year-guidance" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">said the same day</a> that its average loaded freight rate rose 22% in the second quarter, and raised its full-year forecast for underlying EBITDA to $10.5 billion to $12.5 billion, from $8 billion to $10 billion.</p>
<h2>Why it matters</h2>
<p>The WCI and a carrier's average rate measure different things. According to Drewry's <a href="https://www.drewry.co.uk/logistics-executive-briefing/logistics-executive-briefing-articles/world-container-index-methodology" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">methodology</a>, the WCI records agreed short-term spot rates for cargo moving now or within weeks, in dollars per 40ft container, including main surcharges such as bunker charges, weighted by volume on each route. A carrier's average covers every trade it serves, is quoted per TEU (half a 40ft box), and blends spot cargo with cargo moving under contracts agreed earlier. The two cannot be compared in level; only their direction and speed can.</p>
<figure><img src="/article-charts/spot-box-rates-up-2-6-times-carriers-realised-rates-lag/c16_change.png" alt="Bar chart of change from a year earlier: the Drewry spot index up 164% between 9 October 2025 and 8 October 2026, Maersk's average loaded freight rate up 22% in the second quarter of 2026, and Hapag-Lloyd's average freight rate up 9%." width="1600" height="900" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><figcaption>Spot index against carriers' realised rates, change from a year earlier. Sources: Drewry via DCN and MTS Insights; Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd second-quarter reports.</figcaption></figure>
<p>That blend likely explains much of the lag (inferred). Cargo moving under contracts agreed when spot rates were lower keeps paying those prices until the contracts are renegotiated, so a spot rally reaches a carrier's revenue slowly and only in part (inferred). The gap is likely to narrow as annual contracts are repriced (inferred).</p>
<p>Higher rates have not meant higher profits everywhere. Hapag-Lloyd's first-half operating profit (EBIT) fell to $18 million from $677 million. The company cited higher transport costs after "the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz from the end of February", along with reroutings and longer transit times.</p>

<h2>What would change this view</h2>
<ul>
<li><em>The weekly index.</em> Drewry publishes the WCI each Thursday. A fall well below $4,297, the August low, would show the summer level was not holding.</li>
<li><em>Third-quarter results.</em> If carriers' realised rates rise faster than in the second quarter, contracts are repricing sooner.</li>
<li><em>Blank sailings.</em> Drewry's cancelled sailings tracker shows whether carriers are still removing capacity to support rates.</li>
</ul>
