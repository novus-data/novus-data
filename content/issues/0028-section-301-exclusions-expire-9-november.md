<h2>What happened</h2><p>On 1 December 2025 the Office of the US Trade Representative (USTR) <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/01/2025-21671/notice-of-product-exclusion-extensions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-tariff-exclusions-on-178-products-lapse-monday-night-unless-ustr-extends-them" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">extended 178 product exclusions</a> from the Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods. The notice says they apply to goods entered "before 11:59 p.m. eastern daylight time on November 9, 2026." Without a further notice, that is when the exclusions end.</p><p>USTR said it extended them after a trade deal between President Trump and President Xi, and after public comments: 147 of the 178 exclusions drew comments supporting an extension, mostly saying the products are still available only in limited quantities outside China and that more time was needed to move sourcing. Only ten of those 147 also drew comments opposing extension. The notice adds that USTR "may continue to consider further extensions."</p><p>As of 6 November 2026 I found no later extension in the Federal Register. The most recent exclusion notices there are technical amendments, <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/02/2026-17925/notice-of-conforming-amendments-to-product-exclusions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-tariff-exclusions-on-178-products-lapse-monday-night-unless-ustr-extends-them" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">one on 2 September</a> and <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/07/2026-20511/notice-of-conforming-amendment-to-product-exclusion-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-tariff-exclusions-on-178-products-lapse-monday-night-unless-ustr-extends-them" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">one on 7 October</a>, that update the codes five exclusions refer to. Neither changes the deadline.</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>The notice ties each exclusion to a date of entry: goods "entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption" before the cutoff keep the exclusion, and goods entered after it do not. So the deadline falls on the day a shipment clears customs, not the day it leaves China or sails. A container that is on the water on 9 November, or that has not yet been entered, would face the duty unless an extension is published.</p><p>That means importers cannot treat sailing dates as the clock. Those with product that qualifies and is already in the US, or arriving in the next few days, have a reason to file entries before the deadline. I think that creates a short rush of customs filings in the last days (inferred), and a possible bump in paperwork at brokers and ports, though I have no data on it.</p><p>[ALEX: add one or two sentences in your own words on why a lapse might matter beyond the importer: for example, that the exclusions cover goods with few non-Chinese suppliers, per the comments, so a duty is harder to avoid by switching suppliers quickly. Keep it as a mechanism and say how sure you are.]</p><h2>Who it reaches</h2><p><b>Importers of solar manufacturing equipment (reported).</b> Fourteen of the 178 exclusions, under heading 9903.88.70, cover equipment used to make solar products. Entries after the deadline face the Section 301 duty unless extended. Source: <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/01/2025-21671/notice-of-product-exclusion-extensions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-tariff-exclusions-on-178-products-lapse-monday-night-unless-ustr-extends-them" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">USTR notice, 1 December 2025</a>.</p><p><b>Importers of the other 164 excluded products (estimated).</b> These exclusions, under heading 9903.88.69, are listed by tariff subheading in the notice's Annex A. Whether a given importer is exposed depends on whether its product matches an exclusion's description exactly, and I cannot judge that for any one company. Source: same notice.</p><p><b>Importers of the five amended products (inferred).</b> Five exclusions now refer to the statistical codes that took effect on 1 July 2026, four amended on 2 September and one on 7 October. Entries filed under the old codes may not qualify, which could matter even if the deadline is extended. Sources: the <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/02/2026-17925/notice-of-conforming-amendments-to-product-exclusions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-tariff-exclusions-on-178-products-lapse-monday-night-unless-ustr-extends-them" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2 September</a> and <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/07/2026-20511/notice-of-conforming-amendment-to-product-exclusion-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to?utm_source=alexs-newsletter-408c44.beehiiv.com&amp;utm_medium=newsletter&amp;utm_campaign=china-tariff-exclusions-on-178-products-lapse-monday-night-unless-ustr-extends-them" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">7 October</a> notices.</p><p>I am not naming individual companies. The notices list products, not importers, and I have no source tying a named company to any one exclusion.</p><h2>What would change this view</h2><ol><li><p><b>An extension notice.</b> If USTR publishes one before 11:59 p.m. on 9 November, most of this stops mattering.
