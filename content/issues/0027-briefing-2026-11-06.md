About 250 watchmen are involved, at terminals the association says handle about 85% of Long Beach cargo and 33% of Los Angeles cargo. The previous deadline was 5 p.m. Pacific on 4 November. A stoppage would halt gate and yard work, so importers would feel it first as delay and storage fees rather than freight rates (inferred). Next: midnight Pacific, 3 a.m. Eastern on Saturday 7 November.</p><p><i>Eugen Maersk, 2008 file photo. Credit: Qw345, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0</i></p><h2>CONTAINER SHIPPING AND FREIGHT RATES</h2><h3>Maersk raises 2026 profit guidance as spot rates ease</h3><p>A.P. Moller-Maersk said on 5 November 2026 that third-quarter EBIT was $3.3bn, against $1.3bn a year earlier, on an average loaded rate of $3,287 per 40-foot-equivalent unit, up 46%. It raised 2026 EBIT guidance to $8.0bn to $8.5bn from $4.5bn to $6.5bn, and credited the Hormuz closure and East Asian congestion for higher rates. Shares were down 11% in morning trading after EBITDA missed an LSEG average; an AL Sydbank analyst told Bloomberg the guidance implies a slightly lower fourth quarter. Reuters notes some analysts expect the disruption to fade as ships return to Suez, which would erode that premium (inferred). Next: Drewry's weekly index.</p><p><i>Strait of Hormuz, satellite image, 4 December 2020. Credit: NASA GSFC MODIS team</i></p><h2>CHOKEPOINTS AND CANAL TRANSITS</h2><h3>Oil falls 8%, then rebounds, as another tanker is struck</h3><p>UKMTO said a tanker was hit by two unidentified projectiles about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, at 23:20 GMT on 4 November 2026; crew status was unconfirmed (Anadolu; Dawn). Brent had fallen more than 8% that day to its lowest settlement since 31 August, then rose 3.88% to $98.29 on 5 November. The same Reuters report put October vessel hits at 35, the most of the war. Marine fuel followed crude: very-low-sulphur fuel fell $67 a tonne in Singapore. Swings this large make quick surcharge cuts unlikely (estimated). Next: the next UKMTO notice.</p><p><i>Container port, Miami. File photo. Credit: James R. Tourtellotte, US CBP</i></p><h2>THE MACRO DATA TRADE MOVES THROUGH</h2><h3>US imports hit a record as suppliers slow again</h3><p>The US trade deficit narrowed to $105.0bn in September 2026 from a revised $105.7bn, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said on 4 November. Imports rose $5.2bn to $426.6bn, a record per Reuters, with capital goods up $11.6bn, led by computers. The same day, the ISM services survey put supplier deliveries at 56.7%, 3.5 points slower than in September. Record inbound volume with longer lead times suggests buyers are importing early and absorbing delay rather than facing a demand slump (inferred).
